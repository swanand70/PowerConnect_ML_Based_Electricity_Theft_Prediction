2. Project Overview 

Problem Statement:  

The  problem  that  I  am  addressing  in  this  project  is  Electricity  theft  and  improving  the 

consumption experience of the consumers of electricity. 

So, traditionally all the electricity distribution boards used to use normal meters to keep track 

of the electricity consumption of consumers.  In those traditional meters, the monthly  usage 

could be tracked only. So, the problem was that some industries or some individuals in rural 

areas  used 

to  bypass 

the  meters  so 

that 

their  consumption 

is  not 

recorded. 

This  problem  is  solved  by  the  smart  meters,  which  record  the  daily  and  hourly  usage  data. 

Based on this data a ML model could be trained that will assist the employees of the electricity 

board in making smart decisions. 

Proposed Solution:  

Using this improvement in technology, I am planning to build a ML model that will train on 

that data and will provide the board employees important theft related insights that will assist 

them in efficient decision making. 

I am also adding a consumer module in this that will assist them based on their usage data. 

It will include usage and cost saving suggestions and billing due date alerts and energy 

optimisation strategies sent to them via notifications. 

3. Review of Related Works 

Existing Solutions:  

1.  Petrlik, Lezama, et al. (2022) in their research paper, Electricity Theft Detecting using 

Machine  Learning  have  said  that  they  tested  5  machine  learning  models  against  the 

SGCC dataset which had approx. 42000 records that time. Their research focused on 

suggesting the best prediction model using Machine Learning in electrical energy theft. 

Their study aims to determine the most effective machine learning models to identify 

fraudulent  consumption  patterns.  During  data  preprocessing  they  handled  missing 

values using interpolation / mean imputation and corrected extreme outliers via 3 sigma 

rules. Using Support Vector Machine (SVM) they obtained an accuracy of 81%, using 

Random Forest they obtained an accuracy of 80%, using K-Nearest Neighbours they 

obtained a accuracy of 79%, using Logistic Regression they obtained an accuracy of 

69% and using Naïve Bayes they obtained an accuracy of 68%. 

2.  In the regions (most remote places in India), that smart meters are not available some 

of the existing solutions include combining computer vision and Machine Learning and 

with the help of rovers or cameras deployed we can check  if they are bypassing the 

electricity meters using hooks directly to the wires. Although this exists, but it is not 

that  suitable  anymore,  if  we  consider  applications  in  industrial  areas  where  theft  is 

considerable. 

3.  Chung  Tsao,  Rahmalia  and  Chyi  Lu  in  their  research  paper  Machine-learning 

techniques for enhancing electricity theft detection considering transformer reliability 

and  supply  interruptions  (2024)  talk  about  the  application  of  advanced  Machine 

Learning techniques to detect electricity theft. They focus on the impact on supply of 

electricity  and  electricity  usage  pattern.  For  that  they  have  used  the  data  analysis 

techniques like decision trees and Support Vector Machines. They are using aggregated 

parameters to enhance detection accuracy. 

4.  Very traditional methods like physical inspection and testing on site directly, is still a 

widely used way to tackle the problem of electricity theft. These methods are slow, but 

they are accurate. We have to perform physical inspections in high-risk scenarios. 

5.  On  the  contrary,  very  modern  deep  learning  methods  have  been  explored  by 

researchers. Some of them include CNN, LSTM and hybrid CNN-LSTM models for 

detecting more complex consumption patterns. 

6.  In  India,  in  the  past  few  year  lots  of  IoT  innovation  had  been  seen  that  combines 

computer vision, YOLO, and other robotic perception concepts along with ML to solve 

the problem of electricity theft. Such robots are costly, but they are multipurpose, they 

can  be  used  for  industrial  inspections  and  substation  inspections  also.  Their  only 

disadvantage is that they are costly.  

Gap Analysis: 

There are quite a few gaps that my project addresses: 

1.  The ML-based systems that I have mentioned above mainly give a theft prediction, 

but do not clearly explain why a consumer was flagged.  

2.  Existing  solutions  mostly  only  identify  suspicious  consumption  instead  of 

supporting the complete process after detection which includes decision advice and 

assistance to the utility employees.  

3.  We  must  classify  consumers  based  on  risk  level  so  that  high-risk  cases  can  be 

handled first.  

4.  Physical  inspection  is  still  an  important  part  of  verification,  but  it  is  often  not 

properly connected with the automated detection system.  

5.  I  am  handling  meter  reading  analysis,  ML  integration,  AI  predictions  (both  for 

employees and consumers), and a dedicated consumer dashboard all at once, on a 

single platform. 

6.  There  is  a  gap  between  research-level  ML  models  and  a  practical  interface  that 

utility employees can actually use for monitoring and taking action. 

4. Novelty & Value Proposition 

The thing that makes this project unique is the fact that, it used the smart meter data to train the 

ML model instead of traditional meters. A ML model could be trained on traditional meter data 

as well but smart meters are a new technology that has been brought into use very recently. It 

lets us analyse the usage data on a daily and hourly basis, and hence produces more accurate 

results. 

Moreover I am integrating a consumer module in it as will which makes it a one stop solution 

for consumers and employees for smart energy management. 

The ML model trained on smart meter data rather than the traditional meter data makes this 

project unique and interesting.  

This  project  will  ease  the  decision-making  process  of  the  electricity  board  employees  by 

flagging the theft risks calculated. Based on the obtained risk analysis they will be able to plan 

inspections only in high and medium risk cases. 

The consumer side of the project will be very helpful as it will provide bill due date alerts, 

suggestions  to  save  energy,  detect  patterns  in  the  consumptions  that  users  may  miss  (by 

patterns, I mean, there might be certain behaviours that might lead to consumers using more 

electricity). 

5. Technical Specifications  

Tech Stack:  

❖  Frontend: React-based web dashboard both for consumers and utility employees. 

❖  Backend: Python with FastAPI for APIs and system logic.  

❖  Database: Stores consumer, meter, prediction and inspection data.  

❖  Machine Learning: Random Forest for electricity theft prediction.  

❖  AI: LLM-based component for generating explanations from the ML prediction and risk 

factors.  

❖  Deployment: Local development/testing environment. 

System Architecture: 

This project will contain multiple modules with each module having its own core functionality: 

❖  Smart meter data is collected and processed by the backend. 

❖  The data is stored and managed in MySQL database. 

❖  The stored data is analysed and processed by the Random Forest ML model to predict 

the electricity theft risk. 

❖  Based off that, the electricity theft risk is calculated and predictions are made by the AI 

using FastAPI backend. I will handle that part of the logical implementation. 

❖  The  AI  model  will  also  generate  explanations  to  the  risk  factors  and  reason  its 

prediction properly. 

❖  After that the high-risk cases will be sent for the field inspection and results will be 

stored  in  the  database.  (If  integration  of  Reinforcement  Learning  is  possible  in  the 

future in this project, it will prove to be a huge advantage, as the ML model will keep 

learning newer patterns constantly and will produce more accurate results).
